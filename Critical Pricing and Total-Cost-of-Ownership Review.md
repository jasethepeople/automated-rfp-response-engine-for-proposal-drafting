# Critical Pricing and Total-Cost-of-Ownership Review

**Reference date:** 20 August 2026.

## Overall finding

The existing commercial section is structurally responsible because it does not invent price, discount, rate, term, or contractual commitments. However, it is **not benchmark-ready** and cannot support a defensible assertion that the bid is commercially competitive. It provides a pricing placeholder but does not define the normalized buying unit, lifecycle cost basis, comparison cohort, or contract-term normalization needed for a valid enterprise-software comparison.

## Material competitiveness gaps

| Gap | Why it prevents a competitiveness conclusion | Required bid evidence |
|---|---|---|
| No commercial numbers | No price, discount, rate card, or aggregate spend is provided. | Bid pricing schedule and approved internal pricing model. |
| No normalized price metric | Unit prices cannot be compared without the price metric and committed quantity. | User, employee, active user, transaction, tenant, asset, data volume, or another RFP-defined unit; included modules and volume tiers. |
| No scope normalization | Implementation, integration, migration, data, security, training, and support can shift cost between vendors. | Statement of work, included deliverables, client dependencies, and third-party assumptions. |
| No term and renewal normalization | A low initial price can be offset by escalation, minimum commitments, overages, or renewal step-ups. | Contract term, billing cadence, price protection, indexation, renewal, overage, and true-up/true-down terms. |
| No lifecycle TCO calculation | License cost alone omits setup, operation, maintenance, downtime, and exit costs. | One-time costs, recurring costs, internal labor, pass-through charges, contingency, data portability, and transition costs. |
| No comparison cohort | Enterprise benchmarks require comparability across product category, geography, deployment model, contract conditions, and buyer scale. | Comparable third-party quotations, procurement benchmark output, or approved incumbent-cost baseline. |

## Recommended decision rule

Do not call the bid "most competitive" or "below market" unless the price is normalized to the same scope, quantity, term, service level, and lifecycle basis as a verifiable comparator. In the absence of comparable data, state the commercial value proposition through price transparency, buyer control over consumption, cost-risk allocation, price protection, and full-lifecycle TCO disclosure instead of asserting market rank.

## TCO basis for the revised proposal

Five-year lifecycle TCO = contract charges + implementation and transition + integration, migration, security, and enablement + recurring operating and support costs + third-party and consumption charges + exit and transition costs. Any risk contingency should be identified separately rather than embedded invisibly in the price.

## Source rationale

The cost taxonomy is consistent with the lifecycle categories identified in CIO (2024) and IBM (2025). The comparability requirements reflect the need to compare subscription pricing, contract terms, licensing models, usage limits, and buyer peer groups, as described by Vertice (accessed 20 August 2026).

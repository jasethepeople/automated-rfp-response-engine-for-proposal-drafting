# Pricing and TCO Benchmark Review Findings

**Reference date:** 20 August 2026.

## Sources reviewed

| Source | Finding applied to the review | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| CIO, *How to calculate TCO for enterprise software* (1 February 2024) | Enterprise-software TCO should consider initial purchase/configuration/installation, ongoing operation and maintenance, and retirement. Relevant cost elements include testing, security integration, training, networking, process re-engineering, data migration, backup, customization, integration, patching/upgrades, performance management, and license monitoring. | Editorial framework; does not provide category-specific price data. |
| IBM, *What Is Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)?* (13 October 2025) | A lifecycle review should consider initial, setup, operating, maintenance, downtime, and end-of-life components. It also identifies switching, lock-in, and data-egress considerations as relevant to lifecycle cost. | Vendor-authored educational material; no neutral numerical price benchmark. |
| Vertice, *SaaS Pricing Benchmarks* (accessed 20 August 2026) | Meaningful SaaS price benchmarking compares subscription cost, contract term, licensing model, renewal terms, billing structure, and usage limits among similar companies or industries. | Vendor marketing page; its underlying peer transaction dataset is not publicly available in this review. |

## Conclusion

The current proposal contains no price, user/usage volume, product category, deployment model, geography, implementation scope, contract term, discounting, escalation, or competitor quotation. Therefore, a quantitative statement that the bid is more competitive than enterprise benchmarks cannot be supported. The revised section should instead (1) introduce a transparent lifecycle-TCO checklist, (2) make pricing comparability contingent on normalized scope and commercial terms, and (3) state the exact buyer-supplied and competitor data required before asserting a price-market position.
